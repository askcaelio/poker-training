? What is equity?

Equity is your probability of winning the hand at showdown if all remaining cards are dealt randomly. We compute it by Monte Carlo: deal thousands of possible runouts, count wins and ties.

The two numbers reveal an important nuance — your equity vs random hands (89%) is generously high. But your equity vs the actual range villain is plausibly holding (89%) is what matters for the decision. The gap is the difference between thinking villain has 'anything' versus 'something they'd play preflop and call a raise with.'

? What is pot odds?

Pot odds are the price the pot is offering. If villain has to call $2 into a $10 pot, they're getting 5-to-1 odds and need only 17% equity to break even on the call.

The reverse is true for us: when we bet, we're giving villain a price. A small bet gives them better odds (they'll call wider); a big bet gives them worse odds (they fold more, but pay more when they catch). The optimal sizing depends on board texture and villain's range.

Edge is your equity advantage over the break-even threshold — bigger edge means the spot is more profitable.

? What is opponent stats?

Four numbers tell most of the story:

VPIP (Voluntarily Put In Pot) — how often they enter pots. Low (<15%) means they only play premium hands; high (>40%) means they play almost anything.

PFR (Pre-Flop Raise) — how often they raise preflop. The gap between VPIP and PFR shows how passive they are — a player with VPIP 30% but PFR 5% is calling a lot but rarely raising (a calling station).

Fold to C-bet — when they call your preflop raise and miss the flop, how often do they fold to your continuation bet? This is your fold equity for c-bet bluffs. 47% means almost half your bluffs work outright.

Aggression Factor — bets+raises divided by calls. AF > 2 is aggressive, AF < 1 is passive.

? What is range narrowing?

Each action a player takes narrows what hands they could plausibly hold. A TAG opens about 18% of starting hands. When they call your preflop raise (rather than 3-bet), they remove the very top of their range (which would 3-bet) and the bottom (which would fold) — leaving roughly the middle.

This narrowing is why equity vs range matters more than equity vs random. By the river, after multiple actions, villain's range can be narrowed to a handful of hands — sometimes you can reason about their exact holdings.

? What is board texture?

Boards have texture — patterns of suits, connectedness, and pairing that change which hands are likely to be strong.

Dry boards (rainbow, disconnected, no pair like K♥ 7♣ 2♦) reward small probing bets — there are few draws to charge, and your strong hands extract thin value most efficiently with smaller sizing.

Wet boards (monotone, connected, paired like 8♠ 7♠ 5♣) deserve bigger bets to charge the many draws available. If villain has a flush draw, you want them paying full price to chase.

? What is SPR?

Stack-to-Pot Ratio is the effective stack (smaller of yours and villain's) divided by the current pot. It tells you how committed you are.

SPR < 2 — you're already heavily invested. Get the rest in with anything decent.

SPR 2-6 — standard depth. Standard play.

SPR > 6 — deep stacks. Be careful committing your stack with marginal hands; one pair on the flop is rarely worth a 100bb pot when stacks are 200bb deep.

SPR fundamentally changes optimal play. The same hand strength is a snap-call at low SPR and a clear fold at high SPR.

? What is fold equity?

Fold equity is the probability your bet causes villain to fold. It's the other half of EV — pure equity assumes you go to showdown, but most pots end before that.

EV(bet) = fold_eq × pot + (1 − fold_eq) × (eq × (pot + 2 × bet) − bet). When fold equity is high, even weak hands become profitable bets (bluffs). When fold equity is low (vs a calling station), only strong hands should bet for value — bluffs don't work.

The trainer estimates fold equity from observed behavior: how often did this villain actually fold to similar bets in past hands?
